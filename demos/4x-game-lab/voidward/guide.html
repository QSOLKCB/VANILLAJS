VOIDWARD is the campaign mode of VANILLAJS’s experimental 4X Lab. Nothing advances until you end the turn. Your game and content stay on your device.

First expedition

  1. Open Campaign workshop, choose a campaign, then select an available empire, seed and difficulty.
  2. Inspect your home system. Use a colony ship to settle a suitable unowned world; colonies produce resources and research.
  3. In Colonies, manage a construction queue. A mine supports growth; a lab advances research; a shipyard constructs and repairs ships.
  4. In Fleets, issue explore, patrol, return or hold orders. Ships at the same star can form a task force, travelling at the slowest member’s speed.
  5. Choose a research field. Read its dependencies and component unlocks, then use Ship designer to make a class. Same-hull refits require an owned, uncontested shipyard and consume resources and work.
  6. Review diplomacy, reports and Campaign operations. Branch choices persist; conquest, economic progress and survival can open different victory routes.

The numbered keys select command views. E ends a turn when no text input or dialog is active. Sound starts only after you enable it; music, effects and reduced motion have separate settings.

Create a campaign in the Visual Workbench

  1. Open the editor’s 4X campaign panel. Load a shipped template or a native campaign pack. Its draft has separate undo and recovery from the scene.
  2. Edit supported text files and apply them. Validation reports the file, physical line and field. A rejected change keeps the last valid draft.
  3. Send the map to the viewport. Move star-N entities with the existing transform tools, then apply viewport positions. One Workbench X/Z unit equals two campaign X/Y units. Lanes and empire homes have explicit controls; moving a star never guesses a connection.
  4. Select scene geometry and preview its ship silhouette. Apply it to a hull only after checking the projection and source declarations.
  5. Export the native pack, reimport it and choose Play 4X draft. The game opens with that validated draft. In its workshop, Open in Visual Editor sends a draft back.

Hull conversion takes the convex X/Z outline. Height, concave notches, materials and physics are not ship mechanics. Full attribution survives in the optional hull bundle; native scene projects keep their existing strict format.

Text remains the source of truth

Names, empires, planets, hulls, components, technology, buildings, maps, rules, starting fleets, objectives, AI preferences and supported events are editable data. Begin with Grand Frontier’s scenario, objectives and events. The complete text reference lists supported fields and copyable examples.

Native packs support 500 KB of text across known files. Mod SDK exchange remains limited to 32 KiB with 8192-character strings; use native export for larger campaigns. Workbench projects retain their 256 KiB, 128-object and 32-asset limits. Campaigns support up to 64 stars, 256 worlds and six empires.

New mechanics require reviewed JavaScript source changes and explicit local registration. Uploaded packs never execute code. The Machine Dawn source hook is installed code, not an import capability.

Save, share and work offline

Use the save dialog to export a campaign with its state. Existing VOIDWARD v1 saves migrate when imported; save envelopes remain compatible. VANILLAJS autosave keys are separate from the standalone game. Back up exported saves before clearing site storage.

Native campaign JSON contains the text files and can be shared without a server. Import it into either workshop, validate, then start a game. Keep the pack ID stable when updating a universe and raise its version. Source-extension saves require the matching installed source version.

The packaged GitHub Pages build caches local assets under the site subpath. Open it online once before relying on offline reload. Browser storage can be disabled or full; exported files remain the fallback. Save limits and the player manual cover recovery and controls.
